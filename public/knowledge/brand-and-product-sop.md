4. What We Actually Sell
We are not selling windows. Almost anyone can sell a window. We are selling a finished job, from an accountable company.

A customer normally finds a local fabricator through a contractor or a neighbor. That fabricator buys profile from somewhere, uses whatever hardware is cheapest that month, welds in a
small workshop with no fixed process and sends a labor team he does not employ to install. There is no written warranty and no company to call in year three. If that fabricator shuts down
or moves, the customer windows have no support.

That is the actual risk we remove. Not “you have to deal with too many vendors” (most customers deal with just one). The risk is that the one they deal with is unstandardized, informal, and
may not exist in five years.

Part B: The Product
8. uPVC in 90 Seconds
uPVC is rigid plastic: engineered, not ordinary. It stands for unplasticised polyvinyl chloride. Ordinary PVC is soft and bendy, like a pipe. uPVC is hard, dimensionally stable and made to hold
a structural shape for decades.

Why the market moved to it: it doesn’t rust, rot, warp or need painting. It seals well against heat, noise, dust and rain, and a properly made uPVC window is a 25 to 30-year decision.

The one technical fact worth knowing: the profile is hollow on purpose. Those sealed air chambers block heat and sound. Strength comes from galvanised steel reinforcement inside the
frame. It is not “just plastic”. It is a steel-cored frame with an insulating shell.

8.1 The four questions you will get
Keep these short: two sentences, then move the conversation forward.

        It’s plastic. Won’t it break or bend?
         “It’s not ordinary plastic, Sir. It’s a rigid engineered material, and there’s galvanised steel running inside the frame. The strength comes from the steel; the uPVC around it keeps the
         heat and noise out.”
        Will it turn yellow?
         “That used to happen with low-grade material years ago. Ours is UV-stabilised and designed for Indian sun, which is one reason we design our own profile. You also have a 15-year
         warranty on the profile.”
        Will it expand or crack in the heat?
         “No, that’s accounted for in how it’s installed. Our team leaves a controlled gap around the frame and seals it, so the frame can move naturally without any effect on the window.”

11. Site Readiness: The One Thing You Must Get Right
Everything downstream depends on it: whether the lead is C1 or C2, whether Sales visits this week or in four months, and whether we waste a trip.

11.1 What “ready” means
A site is ready when the openings are built and plastering is done. We measure the finished opening, not a rough one.

11.2 How construction runs
        Foundation / footing. What’s happening: Groundwork. Where windows sit: Very early, usually 6+ months away. What the agent does: Capture the requirement, qualify basics, set a
         future follow-up.
        Slab and structure. What’s happening: Columns and RCC slabs going up. Where windows sit: Early, around 4 to 6 months away. What the agent does: Capture details and keep
         following up on the construction timeline.
        Brickwork / masonry. What’s happening: Walls going up, openings created. Where windows sit: Getting closer, around 2 to 4 months. What the agent does: Qualify fully, confirm
         approximate quantity, set a firm follow-up.
        Plastering. What’s happening: Walls and openings being finished. Where windows sit: This is our window. What the agent does: Qualify and hand over to Sales. Offer the free site
         consultation.
        Granite / marble sill work. What’s happening: Granite or marble being fitted around openings. Where windows sit: Important stage before installation. What the agent does:

17. Discovery: The Six Questions
